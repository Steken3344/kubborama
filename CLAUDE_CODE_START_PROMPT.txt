## Game design for the prototype

Court and pieces (real kubb dimensions, meters):
- Court from config preset (default backyard 6 x 3 m; tournament is 8 x 5 m)
  on flat grass. ORIENTATION IS CRITICAL and easy to get wrong: the
  BASELINES are the SHORT sides — the 5 kubbs stand evenly spaced along
  the short (3 m / 5 m) edge, and the LONG dimension (6 m / 8 m) is the
  throwing distance between the baselines. Player stands behind one
  baseline; the king stands at the court's center point.
- Court markings, authentic style (per reference photos in PICS/): real
  kubb courts have NO painted lines — just 4 corner stakes (hompinnar,
  thin wooden pins, often red-topped) and sometimes 2 centerline stakes.
  Default: corner stakes only. Painted/faint dotted lines are an OPTIONAL
  toggle in settings (on = beginner-friendly, off = authentic).
- King: 9 x 9 x 30 cm, center of court.
- Kubbs: 7 x 7 x 15 cm, five evenly spaced along the far baseline.
- Throwing sticks (kastpinnar): 4.4 cm diameter, 30 cm long, six of them
  LYING ON THE GRASS in front of the player's baseline, loosely scattered
  like in a real kubb game (not in a rack).

First-run experience (the whole POC IS this): the app starts DIRECTLY in
the garden in free-throw mode — no menu, no lobby, nothing to click
through. You put on the headset, you're standing outdoors on grass behind
your baseline, six sticks lie at your feet, five kubbs and the king stand
across the court, and you just start throwing. The settings panel and HUD
exist but never gate entry. Open sky, garden horizon (trees/fence at the
edges) — it must FEEL like standing outside even though you're in a room.

Core loop (prototype scope ONLY):
1. Player picks up a stick with either controller: direct grab (bend down,
   grip button) AND distance grab — point at a stick on the ground and
   pull it to the hand (IWSDK's DistanceGrabbable, verified API). Distance
   grab matters for comfort: constantly crouching to the floor in VR gets
   old fast, but bending down must still work for players who want the
   real kubb feeling.
2. Player throws it — and the throw physics must be HONEST in three
   specific ways:
   a. END GRIP: the hand holds the stick at one END (grip anchor in the
      last ~8 cm), never at the center. This is what makes the
      end-over-end flip emerge naturally from the swing — anchor at the
      center and all rotation feel dies. Configure the grab component's
      handle/anchor for this (spike the IWSDK handle API if unclear).
   b. CORRECT RELEASE VELOCITY (the classic VR throwing bug): the stick's
      center of mass is offset from the hand, so on release compute
        v_com = v_hand + ω_hand × (p_com − p_hand)
      and set { linearVelocity: v_com, angularVelocity: ω_hand } via
      PhysicsManipulation. Copying raw controller velocity to the CoM
      makes every throw feel subtly wrong. This math lives in the pure
      core (throwRelease.ts) and is heavily unit-tested with recorded
      controller sample series.
   c. RELEASE TIMING IS THE SKILL: releasing at the right point of the
      swing arc is what the player practices. Frame-average the release
      velocity over a SHORT window (~3-5 frames, weighted toward the most
      recent) — enough to kill sensor noise, never so much that release
      timing feels laggy or "late".
   d. SPIN STYLES MUST BOTH WORK — this is decisive for throw feel.
      Real kubb players split into styles: flat/no-spin throws (Erik's
      style) and heavy-BACKSPIN throws. The spin a player puts on the
      stick decides its orientation at arrival (broadside = big hit
      area) and how it behaves on ground contact (backspin "dies" or
      kicks back; forward roll tumbles onward). Requirements:
      - NO spin assist or normalization by default: the wrist's actual
        angular velocity at release is what the stick gets — different
        styles must EMERGE from the physics, never be scripted.
      - Correct moment of inertia: the physics shape must be a capsule/
        cylinder with the stick's real dimensions and mass so spin
        behaves like a wooden stick, not a generic box.
      - angularDamping is 0 by default in the engine — expose an
        "angular damping in flight" tunable in the tuning lab (air
        resistance on rotation is small but real), alongside the
        existing spin multiplier.
      - Magnus effect (spin-induced lift) is consciously OMITTED in the
        POC — negligible for a dense wooden stick; note it in
        docs/DECISIONS.md so nobody "fixes" it later without data.
      - Ground-contact behavior under spin (backspin kick-back vs
        forward tumble) comes from friction + restitution — tune these
        against BOTH styles, not just one.
      - The M2 calibration gate must record BOTH styles: Erik's flat
        throws AND a set of deliberate backspin throws (Erik can fake
        the style, or a friend supplies it). Golden regression tests
        then cover two profiles — flat and backspin — each with
        expected outcome ranges. Never tune one style without re-running
        the other's goldens.
   Underhand detection: kubb rules require vertical underhand throws
   (end-over-end flip; "helicopter" spin is illegal). Implement a pure-core
   classifier — inputs: the last ~0.5 s of hand poses + release velocity;
   output: style (underhand / overhand / helicopter) + a 0-100 "flip
   quality" score (how well the spin axis is horizontal and perpendicular
   to the throw direction). Show the result after each throw: a HUD badge
   ("Underhand ✓" / "Helicopter!") and a flip-quality meter in the tuning
   lab. Do NOT reject illegal throws in the POC — inform, never punish
   (rule enforcement is a later option, as planned).
3. Gravity acts on the stick; a **wind** vector adds a constant lateral
   force while airborne.
4. A kubb hit hard enough topples. Count a kubb as "felled" when its local
   up-axis tilts more than ~60 degrees from world up and it comes to rest.
   Show a simple HUD/scoreboard: sticks thrown, kubbs felled, king felled.
   MICRO-GOAL (what makes the sandbox feel like a game): free-throw mode
   is played in ROUNDS of 6 sticks. The round's goal: fell all 5 kubbs,
   then the king. When the 6th stick settles (or the king falls), show
   the round result — kubbs felled, sticks used, "court cleared!" if all
   6 pieces fell — plus the session's PERSONAL BEST, then auto-reset for
   the next round after a few seconds. No rules engine, no turns — just
   purpose, a reason to throw again, and a number to beat.
   STATS & PERSONAL BESTS (core/stats.ts, pure core, fed by the same
   event bus as scoring — one source of truth, DRY):
   - Per round: kubbs felled, sticks used, court cleared or not.
   - Personal bests (persisted): fewest sticks to clear the court, most
     kubbs felled in a round, longest streak of cleared rounds, LONGEST
     THROW (release point → landing point, meters — from the telemetry
     the throw system already records), and longest FELLING throw (the
     greatest distance at which a throw toppled a kubb — the bragging
     stat).
   - Lifetime totals (persisted): rounds played, total throws, total
     kubbs felled, kings felled, courts cleared, TOTAL PLAY TIME (count
     active in-XR time, not page-open time; shown as h:mm), and HIT
     ACCURACY — throws that felled at least one piece / total throws,
     shown as a percentage both lifetime and for the current round.
   - Persistence in localStorage behind a VERSIONED zod schema (schema
     version field + migration path), because the stats shape WILL grow:
     the schema reserves a matches section (wins/losses vs AI, vs ghosts,
     vs live opponents) AND an optional userId field — a future store-
     installed PWA can read Meta's app-scoped user id (Digital Goods API,
     getLoggedInUserId) and it becomes the stable key for stats. Identity
     roadmap details are in IMPLEMENTATION_AND_ASSETS.txt section 10.
   - LOCAL PROFILE: at first run, ask the player for a display name
     (spatial UI keyboard; skippable, default "Player"). Stored with
     settings, editable in the settings panel, shown on the HUD and —
     later — on the multiplayer avatar name tag. No accounts in the POC:
     plain web apps get no Meta identity by design.
   - HUD shows current round + the relevant personal best; a stats tab on
     the settings panel shows the full list (all strings via i18n).
   - PROGRESS TRACKING (skill development over time — "am I getting
     better?"). In the POC (cheap, the data already exists per throw):
       * per-hand accuracy — left vs right hand hit % (fun and useful)
       * average flip quality — the underhand classifier's 0-100 score,
         rolling average of the last 50 throws vs lifetime; the clearest
         "your technique is improving" number
       * form curve — hit accuracy over the last 50 throws vs lifetime
         accuracy, so improvement is visible ("form: 62% ↑ / career 48%")
       * personal-best history — each PB stored with a timestamp, so the
         stats tab can show WHEN records fell (a progression timeline)
     Post-POC (schema reserves fields now, built later — file as issues):
       * grouping radius — spread of landing points around their center,
         the throwing equivalent of shot grouping; consistency IS skill
       * accuracy per distance band (near/mid/far kubbs)
       * first-throw strike rate per round
       * king stats — attempts, hit rate, longest king fell
       * play streaks — days-in-a-row played, sessions per week
       * small achievement badges (first cleared court, 100 kubbs felled,
         perfect round 6-for-6...) — ties into the progression lens
     All stats are LOCAL to the device (localStorage) — no accounts, no
     tracking, nothing leaves the headset. Show only a handful of numbers
     on the HUD; the full list lives in the stats tab. Never let stats
     UI clutter the core throwing experience.
   - Stats derive ONLY from events (RoundEnded, KubbFelled, KingFelled,
     CourtCleared...) and throw telemetry — never counted ad hoc in UI
     code; the stats reducer is unit-tested with event sequences (TDD).
5. Knocking the king counts separately (in real rules, felling the king
   early = instant loss — just display it, no rule enforcement yet).
6. A "reset round" action (controller button + a spatial UI button) puts
   all pieces and sticks back.

Tunables — put these in ONE config module (`src/config.ts`) because later
versions make them player-adjustable options:
- gravity (default 9.81 m/s²), wind vector (default 0), stick mass 0.29 kg,
  kubb mass 0.47 kg, king mass 1.45 kg (density-computed birch defaults —
  see the plan; material preset pine/birch/rubberwood scales them),
  friction/restitution, topple angle
  threshold, named camera poses, and court size as PRESETS (tournament
  8x5 m, backyard 6x3 m, kids 5x2 m — POC uses backyard 6x3 by default,
  it suits a garden and is beginner-friendly).
Environment/background (garden) should also be swappable later — keep the
environment in its own module/entity group.

Out of scope for the prototype (do not build yet): full rule engine, turns,
field kubbs, opponent/AI, multiplayer, hand tracking, sound design beyond
simple hit sounds, menus beyond reset + wind/gravity debug sliders, and the
spectator/grandstand view, avatars and realtime multiplayer. BUT: a future
spectator mode (third-person side view on external screens) AND realtime
multiplayer with visible avatars (opponent's head/hands live, voice chat,
avatar picked at start) are both planned, and both are fed by the same
state-sync architecture. Follow the four cheap preparation rules from
IMPLEMENTATION_AND_ASSETS.txt (sections 8 and 10): keep simulation state

## Portability rules — keep the Godot door open (see GODOT_PLAN_B.txt)

A native Godot port is the planned Plan B. These POC rules make that
migration cheap; none of them cost meaningful effort now:

- **Everything valuable lives in engine-neutral JSON, not in code.**
  Config values, court presets, 0-100 tuning mappings, tuning presets,
  i18n dictionaries, stats/settings schemas, ghost recordings, and —
  most importantly — the GOLDEN THROW RECORDINGS (controller pose
  series + expected outcome ranges) are all stored as JSON files that
  the TS modules LOAD, never as TS literals. Godot reads JSON natively:
  the calibration data becomes the port's acceptance-test suite, and
  Erik's recorded throws replay against Jolt physics unchanged.
- **Boring TypeScript in the pure core.** Core modules use plain
  functions and plain data — no fancy generics, no decorators, no
  TS-exotica — and every non-obvious algorithm carries its math in a
  comment (v_com = v_hand + ω × r etc.). Translation to GDScript must
  be mechanical, not archaeological.
- **Assets stay in standard formats** (GLB, KTX2, HDR, OGG/WAV) that
  Godot imports natively — never convert anything INTO an IWSDK-only
  format that isn't derived from a standard source in the repo.
- **Scene layout derives from config**, never hand-authored in
  IWSDK-specific scene files beyond what the scaffold requires — the
  court builds itself from piecePositions(preset) in any engine.

## Debug & observability (built in M1, not bolted on later)

The app must be easy to debug for BOTH of us — and "both" includes you:
everything below is designed so you can read the app's behavior through
your MCP tools without a human relaying what happened.

- **Structured logger** (pure core, core/log.ts): no raw console.log
  anywhere. Every message goes through the logger with a level and a
  channel tag — [throw], [physics], [grab], [state], [audio], [net],
  [i18n], [settings]. Consistent prefix format, because
  browser_get_console_logs supports pattern filtering: the tags exist so
  you can pull exactly the channel you're debugging.
- **Global error capture from M0**: window.onerror + unhandledrejection +
  XR session failures are caught, logged with stack + the last ~20 log
  lines of context, and — critically — shown IN-HEADSET as a red error
  toast (the console is invisible when you're wearing the headset; errors
  must never vanish silently). Error capture ships in production too;
  the rest of the debug UI is dev-only.
- **In-VR debug overlay** (toggle on the debug panel): FPS/frame time,
  each stick's state-machine state, last throw's telemetry summary,
  active tuning preset, last 5 log lines.
- **State dump**: one command (console + reachable via MCP) that dumps
  the full game state as a single JSON blob — piece transforms and
  states, config values, settings, version. Pairs with your ecs_snapshot/
  ecs_diff tools for frame-by-frame physics debugging.
- **Debug report export**: one action bundles last N logs + errors +
  throw telemetry + active preset + versions into one JSON for pasting
  into our chat. When Erik says "something felt wrong", this file is the
  answer to "what happened?".

Hand tracking note: the scaffold enables handTracking as an optional XR
feature and the input layer supports it — leave it enabled, but
controllers stay the primary throwing input (bare-hand release precision
isn't good enough for throw feel yet; revisit post-POC per the backlog).

## Working rules

- All code comments, identifiers, README and git commit messages in
  **English**. Talk to me in **Swedish** in the chat.
- **TypeScript strict** from M0: "strict": true + noUncheckedIndexedAccess
  + exactOptionalPropertyTypes; typescript-eslint with no-explicit-any as
  error; prettier.
- **Functional core, imperative shell** — the testability rule: ALL game
  logic (topple math, scoring reducer, state machines, court layout, wind
  force, throw-release math from controller samples, config, replay/ghost
  encoding) lives in pure TS modules that import NOTHING from three.js/
  IWSDK/Havok — plain data in, plain data out. Rendering, XR input and
  physics are thin adapters around that core. This lets most of the game
  be unit-tested in Node without a headset.
- **TDD for the pure core — tests come FIRST.** Red → green → refactor:
  for every pure-core function or behavior change, write the failing
  vitest test before the implementation, make it pass, then clean up.
  Commit so the sequence is visible (test + impl in small commits).
  The pragmatic boundary: TDD applies to the functional core (where
  inputs/outputs are plain data); adapters (rendering, XR input, Havok
  bindings) are NOT test-driven — they're covered by the golden-throw
  regression and emulator/MCP verification instead. Use fast-check for
  a few property-based tests where they pay off. The throw-release math
  (frame-averaged velocity + lever-arm term from recorded controller
  samples) is the most important unit under test in the project.
  Each milestone's DoD still includes: pure-core logic covered, and
  typecheck + lint + tests green in CI.
- **DRY — every piece of knowledge lives in exactly ONE place.** Piece
  dimensions, masses, the 0-100 tuning mappings, court presets, topple
  threshold, i18n strings, event names, zod schemas: defined once
  (config.ts / core modules) and imported everywhere else — never
  re-typed, never copy-pasted. When the same LOGIC appears a second
  time, extract it then and there. But DRY applies to knowledge, not to
  incidental similarity: don't force two things that merely look alike
  today into one premature abstraction — duplication is cheaper than
  the wrong abstraction. The review gate already checks "config values
  defined once" and "no duplicated logic"; treat violations as findings.
- **Determinism**: no Math.random() in game code — inject a seedable RNG.
  SI units everywhere (meters, seconds, radians, kg); suffix ambiguous
  names (speedMps, angleRad). Golden physics tests assert ranges, never
  exact positions.
- **zod** at untrusted boundaries (persisted config now; peer messages
  and ghosts later): types check compile time, zod checks runtime.
- **i18n from day one** — the game is called **KubbOrama** and the UI must
  support multiple languages. NO hardcoded user-facing strings anywhere:
  every string goes through a small typed i18n module in the pure core
  (a `t(key)` function over typed dictionaries; missing-key = type error).
  Ship Swedish and English from the start; default to the browser
  language, fall back to English; the language setting lives in the M4
  settings panel and persists. Verify early that the spatial UI font
  atlas includes å ä ö Å Ä Ö — MSDF/glyph atlases often omit them, and
  it's cheaper to catch in M1 than after all UI is built.
- Small, frequent commits (conventional commits style), one logical change
  per commit.
- MERGE into the scaffold-generated `CLAUDE.md` (never create a new one,
